Create investment calculator simulator con:
Input Data

Basic (essential)

Initial capital: the amount you start with.
Periodic contributions: the amount you add on a regular basis (can be zero).
Contribution frequency: monthly, quarterly, annually, etc.
Expected annual interest rate or return: expressed as a percentage.
Time horizon: the duration of the investment in years (or months).
Compounding frequency: daily, monthly, quarterly, or annually. This determines how often interest is reinvested.

Advanced (those that make a real difference)

Timing of contributions: whether they occur at the beginning or the end of each period, as this affects the outcome.
Estimated inflation: used to show the real value (purchasing power) in addition to the nominal value.
Fees and expenses: for example, the annual cost of a fund (TER), which can significantly reduce returns over the long term.
Taxes: the rate applicable to gains (in Spain, for instance, savings are taxed in brackets).
Annual increase in contributions: for example, increasing them by 3% each year.
Periodic withdrawals: useful for simulating a retirement phase.
Results
Final capital: the total accumulated by the end of the time horizon.
Total contributed: the amount you have invested from your own pocket.
Interest earned: the difference between the final capital and the total contributed, illustrating the effect of compound interest.
Inflation-adjusted value: the equivalent value in today’s currency.
Total return and equivalent APR/IRR (Annual Percentage Rate / Internal Rate of Return).
Visualization
Growth chart: stacked bars or an area chart that distinguishes between contributed capital and interest, year by year. This is the most instructive element, as it clearly shows the exponential curve.
Breakdown table: a year-by-year or month-by-month view, showing the opening balance, contributions, interest earned, and closing balance.
Scenario comparison: for example, comparing different return outcomes (pessimistic, baseline, and optimistic). Reference formula

For an initial principal without additional contributions:

A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n·t)

where P is the initial principal, r is the annual interest rate, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the number of years. When periodic contributions are included, the term for the series of payments is added:

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) + PMT × [((1 + r/n)^(nt) − 1) / (r/n)]

Best design practices
Data validation: prevent negative values ​​or empty fields, and provide clear error messages.
Real-time results: ensure results update dynamically when a slider is adjusted.
Appropriate formatting: display as currency (euros), use thousands separators, and limit decimal places.
Legal disclaimer: clearly state that this is a simulation and that past performance does not guarantee future results.
